# Scanned books: S0 findings

Package S0 of "Scanned books" (`docs/DESIGN.md`, `docs/SCANNED_BOOKS_PLAN.md`). Established on 16
November 2026 with Xcode 27.2 (27B52), the iPhoneOS/iPhoneSimulator 27.2 SDKs, the iOS 27.0
simulator runtime (iPad Pro 11-inch (M5), the device `scripts/sim.sh` picks) and macOS 26.7.3, on
an Apple M1 Max host. Nothing here was run on an iPad or on iOS 17.

How each answer was established is marked:

- **[sim]** measured in the iOS 27 simulator: either a throwaway app-hosted test that opened
  `scanned.pdf` in a real `ReaderSession` hosted in a window (the reader's real `PDFView`, page
  overlays and feature controllers), or the real app launched on the book and driven by hand,
  with temporary debug logging in `ReaderPDFView.buildMenu`, the selection-changed notification
  and `HighlightController.highlight`. All probes have been removed again.
- **[mac]** measured with a scratch macOS Swift program (macOS PDFKit and Vision).
- **[sdk]** read from the SDK headers or `.swiftinterface`.
- **[code]** read from this repository.
- **[inferred]** reasoned from the above, not observed.
- **[device]** could not be established without a real iPad.

## Headline

**A Live Text selection on a scanned page can be read by the app, and reaches it exactly as a
normal selection does.** On this SDK PDFKit itself recognises the text of scanned pages shown in a
`PDFView` and adds it to those in-memory `PDFPage` objects as an ordinary text layer. From then on
the page behaves as if the PDF had text: `PDFPage.string`, `numberOfCharacters`,
`selection(for:)`, `selectionForWord(at:)`, `selectionsByLine()` and `PDFDocument.findString`
all work on it, and a finger selection is a normal `PDFSelection` that arrives through the
reader's existing selection-menu path as a `ReaderTextSelection` with per-line rects and a
string. Highlight, Add as Heading, Copy, Look Up and Select All all appear on it. **[sim]**

The text exists only on the **on-screen** `PDFDocument`, only for pages that have been shown,
only after a delay of seconds, and not in the file: a second `PDFDocument` opened on the same
file (as the search worker opens) has no text, and a new session starts without it. **[sim]**

S4 can therefore go ahead, but much of it already works through the existing highlight code and
the description should change (see "Consequences for S1 to S4").

## Deliverable 1: the `scanned.pdf` fixture

Generated by `Fixtures/make-fixtures.swift` (`Scanned`, `scannedPageImage`, `writeScanned`,
`verifyScanned`) into `Fixtures/out/scanned.pdf`, never committed.

- 30 pages, US Letter (612 × 792 pt), no outline.
- **Page 1** is a typed cover with a real text layer: "The Scanned Fixture Book", "A typed cover
  on a scanned book", "Fixture page 1".
- **Pages 2 to 30** are each exactly one greyscale image XObject drawn over the whole page, with
  no font and no text: a printed page (9 pt Helvetica running header "Scanned Fixture Book |
  Chapter n" with a rule, four paragraphs of 11 pt Times body set in a 468 pt measure, footer
  "Scanned page n") rendered to a bitmap at 300 dpi (2550 × 3300 px) and then drawn. Body text is
  the same size as the typed fixtures, i.e. ordinary book print size.
- **Known phrase:** `The quiet heron measures tidewater.` alone on the first body line of
  **page 17** (zero-based index 16), left margin 72 pt, baseline at page-space y = 696.
  `Scanned.phraseBox` (x 68, y 690, 260 × 20 pt) is a generous box it falls inside. Measured
  boxes for it: Vision accurate (71, 692, 163 × 12); PDFKit's own Live Text line
  (72, 693, 159 × 12).
- File size 7.6 MB; generation adds about 2 s to the generator.
- The generator verifies on macOS: 30 pages; the cover has text and no image; every other page
  has empty `PDFPage.string`, exactly one XObject and no Font resource; `findString` of the
  phrase finds nothing. `Tests/ScannedFixtureTests.swift` checks the same on iOS PDFKit
  (`numberOfCharacters == 0` on pages 2 to 30) and passes on the iOS 27 simulator.
- Wired in like the others: `scripts/fixtures.sh` regenerates when `scanned.pdf` is missing;
  `UITests/Support/Fixtures.swift` has `Fixtures.scanned` (for `-importFixture`); app tests load
  it from `Fixtures/out/scanned.pdf` the way they load `small.pdf` (for example
  `SearchTestSupport.makeFixture("scanned.pdf", pages: 30)`, which is how the probes opened it).
- Limitation: the pages are clean, unskewed, noise-free renders. Real scans (skew, noise, JPEG
  or JBIG2 compression, two columns, rotated pages) are not represented.

## Live Text in `PDFView`

### 1. Does Live Text run in the simulator? Is there an API to turn it on or off?

**Yes, it runs in the iOS 27 simulator. [sim]** Opening `scanned.pdf` in a hosted
`ReaderSession` at page 1 and jumping to page 17, page 17's `numberOfCharacters` went from 0 to
2,318 after **6.4 s** (first use in the process, so this includes any model loading); by then
pages 16 to 18 (visible) and page 2 (visible at open) had text, and 3 s later page 18 too.
Jumping to page 28, pages 27 to 29 gained text within 5 to 15 s. Pages never shown stayed
without text. Pages keep their text after scrolling away, for the life of that `PDFDocument`.
It also happened in **markup mode** (`isInMarkupMode = true`): pages 7 to 9 gained text. In the
running app (book opened with `-openBook scanned -openPage 17`) a long-press selected a word
about 8 s after launch.

`ImageAnalyzer.isSupported` is `true` in the simulator, and VisionKit's `ImageAnalyzer` read
page 17 in 2.1 s and found the phrase. **[sim]** No `ImageAnalysisInteraction` or other
VisionKit view or interaction appears anywhere in the `PDFView` hierarchy (only PDFKit's own
`PDFDocumentView`/`PDFPageView`, PencilKit's overlay views and UIKit's standard text
interactions), so the recognition is internal to PDFKit. **[sim]**

**There is no public API to enable, disable, trigger, observe or wait for it. [sdk]** The
PDFKit headers (`PDFView.h`, `PDFPage.h`, `PDFDocument.h`, `PDFSelection.h`) and the PDFKit
`.swiftinterface` mention OCR only in `PDFDocumentSaveTextFromOCROption` (question 6); there is
no Live Text, image-analysis or text-recognition property, and no notification when a page gains
text. **[inferred]** The only way to know that a page has been analysed is to poll the on-screen
page's `numberOfCharacters`.

A second hosted session on the same book in the same process (relaunch-like) did **not** show
text on page 17 within 5 s (0 characters at 0.3 s and at 5 s). Whether that is just a slower
start or a cache keyed on something else was not established. **[sim]**

### 2. Does the selection reach the app as a `ReaderTextSelection`?

**Yes, unchanged. [sim]** In the running app, a long-press on "heron" on page 17:

- posted `PDFViewSelectionChanged` on the reader's `PDFView`; `view.currentSelection` was a
  normal `PDFSelection` with string "heron", page 17;
- the first responder was PDFKit's `PDFDocumentView`, as for any PDF text selection;
- `ReaderPDFView.buildMenu(with:)` ran with `builder.system == .context`, and
  `ReaderPDFController.currentSelection` returned a `ReaderTextSelection` with one part:
  page index 16, `lineRects` [(116, 693, 25 × 12)], string "heron";
- word, multi-word and multi-line selections behave as on a typed page. A programmatic
  two-line selection gave one rect per line with the line's text: (72, 693, 159 × 12) "The quiet
  heron measures tidewater." and (72, 669, 303 × 13) "Independent factorization vector …".

The rects are in page space and line up with the image text (compare Vision's box for the same
line, question 8). The text is PDFKit's recognition and can contain errors: on page 17 it read
"sımilar" (dotless ı) where Vision's accurate request read "similar". **[sim]**

What the app **cannot** observe **[sim, sdk]**: that the selection came from recognised rather
than file text (nothing on `PDFSelection` or `PDFPage` says so), when a page has been analysed
(no callback), and the recognised text of pages that have not been shown. **[inferred]** To tell a
"Live Text" selection from a file-text one, the app must ask whether that page has text in the
file, using a `PDFDocument` other than the on-screen one (the on-screen page now reports text) or
the S1 detection/recognition records.

### 3. The highlight colour menu, and what happens when a colour is chosen

**The menu appears and the existing code stores and draws a normal highlight. [sim]** The edit
menu showed Highlight, Add as Heading, Copy, Select All and ">" (the full menu, from the
`buildMenu` log: the app's inline group of Highlight and Add as Heading, then Copy, Select All,
Scan Text, Look Up, Translate, Search Web, Share…). Highlight opened the four colours; choosing
Green on a selection of "tidewater" stored, through `HighlightController.highlight` and
`HighlightEditing.apply`:

```
p17 rects [(188, 693, 40 × 12)] text 'tidewater'
```

It was drawn at once (green fill exactly over the word in the image), and **after a relaunch
the highlight was drawn within 3 s of launch**, before PDFKit had re-recognised the page
(screenshot), because `HighlightAnnotation` draws its stored rects and never consults page text.
In the hosted test the same path stored "The quiet heron measures tidewater." with rect
(72, 693, 159 × 12), added a `HighlightAnnotation` with that rect, and a new session on the store
applied the annotation to page 17 at 0.3 s while the page still had 0 characters. **[sim]**

The stored record is indistinguishable from a text-layer highlight (no source field exists yet;
see question 10).

### 4. Copy, Look Up, Add as Heading and paragraph Select All

All operate on the selection as on a typed page:

- **Copy: works. [sim]** Copy on "heron" put `heron` on the simulator pasteboard
  (`xcrun simctl pbpaste`).
- **Select All (paragraph): works. [sim]** On "heron" (the phrase is a paragraph of its own) it
  grew to "The quiet heron measures tidewater."; on "show" in the second line of the next
  paragraph it grew to the whole six-line paragraph, and nothing else.
  `ParagraphSelection.lines(on:)` found 27 lines on the analysed page. (After Select All the edit
  menu did not reappear on a tap in the simulator; that matches what the reader does on typed
  pages and was not investigated further.)
- **Add as Heading: offered. [sim]** It is in the menu (it requires a non-empty
  `selection.string`, which the selection has). It was not tapped through to a saved heading.
  **[inferred]** It works, since it only uses `selection.string` and the page index.
- **Look Up: offered. [sim]** Look Up, Translate and Search Web are in the overflow menu. The
  simulator's injected taps on the overflow list were unreliable, so Look Up was not seen
  opening. **[inferred]** It works, since the selection is an ordinary text selection with a
  string. On the device checklist.

### 5. Does `PDFPage.string` stay empty? Does the app's search find anything?

- **On the on-screen document, no: it fills in.** After PDFKit has analysed a shown page,
  that `PDFPage`'s `string` is the recognised text (2,318 characters on page 17) and
  `PDFDocument.findString("heron")` on the on-screen document returns 1 hit. **[sim]**
- **On any other `PDFDocument` of the same file, yes: it stays empty** (`numberOfCharacters` 0
  on page 17 of a fresh `PDFDocument(url:)` opened while the on-screen page had text;
  `findString` 0). A new session's document also starts with none. **[sim]**
- **The app's search finds nothing on scanned pages.** In the running app with page 17 shown
  and analysed, searching "heron" gave "No Results". `PDFTextSearch` reads page text from private
  `PDFDocument`s opened from the file (`App/Features/Search/SearchEngine.swift`, `PageSource`), so
  PDFKit's recognition never reaches it. **[sim, code]** S2 is needed as designed.

### 6. `saveTextFromOCR`

**Exists. [sdk]** `PDFDocumentSaveTextFromOCROption` in `PDFDocument.h`,
`PDFKIT_AVAILABLE(13_0, 16_0)` (macOS 13.0, iOS/iPadOS 16.0), Swift name
`PDFDocumentWriteOption.saveTextFromOCR` (`PDFKit.apinotes`). Header comment: when enabled, OCR is
performed on all pages on which it has not been done already and saved as invisible, selectable
text. Available at the 17.0 floor without a check. Information only; not used, and not tried.

## Vision

### 7. Which request at the iPadOS 17.0 floor; is a newer one worth a gated path?

**[sdk]** At the floor: **`VNRecognizeTextRequest`** (iOS 13.0) with `recognitionLevel =
.accurate` and revision 3 (`VNRecognizeTextRequestRevision3`, iOS 16.0; the default and only
non-deprecated revision; revisions 1 and 2 are deprecated from iOS 18). Word boxes come from
`VNRecognizedText.boundingBox(for: Range)` on each line's top candidate. `automaticallyDetectsLanguage`
(iOS 16) and `supportedRecognitionLanguages()` are available. 30 languages are supported for the
accurate level (measured on macOS).

Newer, in the SDK:

- **`RecognizeTextRequest`** (Swift-only Vision API, iOS 18.0): the same engine (its only
  revision is `.revision3`), `async` and `Sendable`, same options. It adds nothing the app needs.
  **Not worth a gated path.**
- **`RecognizeDocumentsRequest`** (iOS 26.0, revision 1): returns a `DocumentObservation` with
  structure: `document.text` with `lines` and **`words`** (word-level
  `RecognizedTextObservation`s with boxes), `paragraphs`, `title`, `tables`, `lists`, detected
  data (dates, links), and barcodes; `RecognizedTextObservation` gains `transcript`, `isTitle`,
  `shouldWrapToNextLine` and `textDirection` on iOS 26. Measured on page 17 in the simulator:
  1.7 s against 1.2 s for `VNRecognizeTextRequest` accurate, 27 lines, 305 words, 8 paragraphs,
  "heron" word box (115, 693, 28 × 10). **[sim]** Search needs words and positions, which the
  floor request already gives, so this is **not needed for version 1**. It would be the one to
  gate if paragraph structure or hyphen joining (`shouldWrapToNextLine`) is wanted later, with
  `VNRecognizeTextRequest` as the fallback.

### 8. Does it run in the simulator? Phrase found? Conversion to page space

**Yes. [sim]** `VNRecognizeTextRequest` (accurate and fast), `RecognizeTextRequest` and
`RecognizeDocumentsRequest` all ran in the iOS 27 simulator, in the app test host.

The phrase was found **only on page 17**, at every configuration except one: accurate at 150, 200
and 300 dpi read it exactly, confidence 1.0; fast read it at 200 and 300 dpi (confidence 0.5) but
misread it at 150 dpi (simulator: "me&sures"; macOS: not found). **[sim, mac]**

**Conversion (verified).** Render the page's crop box, unrotated, into a bitmap whose origin is
the crop box's lower-left corner:

```
let box = page.bounds(for: .cropBox); let s = dpi / 72
context(width: box.width * s, height: box.height * s)
ctx.scaleBy(x: s, y: s); ctx.translateBy(x: -box.minX, y: -box.minY)
page.draw(with: .cropBox, to: ctx)
```

Vision's normalised rects have their origin at the image's lower-left, so a normalised rect `n`
maps to PDFKit page space as

```
CGRect(x: box.minX + n.minX * box.width, y: box.minY + n.minY * box.height,
       width: n.width * box.width,         height: n.height * box.height)
```

with no y flip. Result for the phrase line: (71, 692, 163 × 12) in the simulator and
(71, 693, 162 × 12) on macOS, against PDFKit's own selection rect for the same line,
(72, 693, 159 × 12), and the drawn baseline y = 696. Word boxes ("heron" (114, 693, 28 × 11) in
the simulator) line up with PDFKit's `selectionForWord` rect for the same word,
(116, 693, 25 × 12). Accurate word boxes are full line height; fast word boxes are not (heights
5 to 10 pt), another reason to use accurate.

Not established: **rotated pages** (`/Rotate`) and crop boxes offset from the media box. The
fixture has neither. **[inferred]** For a rotated page the image must be rendered without the
page's rotation (or the rotation undone on the boxes) so boxes stay in unrotated page space,
the space `Highlight.rects` and search rects use; whether `PDFPage.draw(with:to:)` applies
`/Rotate` was not checked. S2 should test a rotated page.

### 9. Time per page and peak memory

**These are simulator and Mac numbers, not iPad numbers.** Both ran on an M1 Max host. The
simulator runs on the Mac's CPU/GPU, not on an iPad's Neural Engine, so iPad times may be better
or worse and must be measured on a device.

Simulator (iOS 27, app test host, 29 scanned pages, `VNRecognizeTextRequest` rev 3,
`usesLanguageCorrection`, en-US), one process, configurations run in this order: **[sim]**

| Level | Render | Render s/page | Recognise s/page | First page | Footprint before → max (rise) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| accurate | 150 dpi | 0.016 | 1.23 | 1.74 | 62 → 248 MB (+186) |
| accurate | 300 dpi | 0.005 | 1.22 | 1.17 | 167 → 254 MB (+88) |
| fast | 150 dpi | 0.015 | 0.39 | 0.44 | 169 → 212 MB (+43) |
| fast | 300 dpi | 0.005 | 0.39 | 0.37 | 177 → 201 MB (+24) |
| accurate | 200 dpi | 0.020 | 1.21 | 1.20 | 177 → 258 MB (+81) |

`phys_footprint` sampled every 5 ms. The first row's rise includes loading the models (they stay
loaded, which is why the baseline climbs); the later rows' rises are the per-page working set
over a warm process: about **80 to 90 MB** for accurate and 25 to 45 MB for fast. The 300 dpi
render is fastest only because the fixture's images are 300 dpi (no resampling); on real
scans render time grows with output size.

macOS (separate process per configuration, so these are clean peaks): **[mac]**

| Level | Render | Recognise s/page | Peak footprint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| accurate | 150 dpi | 0.52 | 190 MB |
| accurate | 200 dpi | 0.55 | 136 MB |
| accurate | 300 dpi | 0.54 | 153 MB |
| fast | 150 dpi | 0.23 | 114 MB |
| fast | 200 dpi | 0.21 | 169 MB |
| fast | 300 dpi | 0.24 | 310 MB |

Reading: recognition time does not depend on render size between 150 and 300 dpi for 11 pt
text; accuracy does at the fast level. **Suggested starting point for S2: accurate, about 200 dpi
(1700 × 2200 px greyscale, a 3.7 MB buffer against 8.4 MB at 300 dpi), one page at a time**,
then tune on a device. In the simulator that is about 1.2 s per page, so a 300-page book is about
6 minutes; the import notice's estimate should come from device measurements.

## 10. What in the existing code S1 to S4 would collide with

1. **The on-screen document gains text that the file does not have.** Anything that asks the
   on-screen `PDFDocument` (`session.document`, `session.pdf.page(at:)`) whether a page has text
   gets "yes" for scanned pages that have been shown. Scan detection, the "which source has text"
   decision in S2 and S4's text-source tagging must read the file through their own
   `PDFDocument` (as `PDFTextSearch` and `ImportPipeline.check` already do) or the S1 records,
   never the on-screen one. (`ReaderSession` already documents that background work opens its
   own instance from `documentURL`.)
2. **S4 "capture" already works through the existing code.** A selection on an analysed page
   goes through `ReaderPDFController.currentSelection` → `HighlightController.selectionMenuElements`
   → `HighlightEditing.apply` and stores rects plus PDFKit's recognised text
   (`App/Features/Highlights/`). The design's case "Live Text reports no text" did not occur: a
   selection cannot exist on an unanalysed page, and `currentSelection` returns `nil` for an empty
   string (`ReaderPDFController.swift`) while `HighlightEditing.apply` skips parts with empty text
   and nothing absorbed. **Drawing from rects alone is already how every highlight is drawn**
   (`HighlightAnnotation.draw` fills `lineRects`; nothing re-matches text). Recolour, delete and
   Copy (from the stored text) need no change. What S4 actually has to add is the source tag
   (decided from the file, item 1), the crop rendering, and the crop in lists and exports.
3. **Merging overlapping highlights reads page text.** `HighlightEditing.mergedLinesAndText`
   rebuilds lines and text with `page.selection(for:)` on the on-screen page. On an analysed
   scanned page this works (it is how the stored text above was produced), and it can only run
   when a new selection exists, i.e. after analysis. The merged text is then PDFKit's
   recognition at that moment, so a merged Live Text highlight's text can change on merge.
4. **Schema.** `HighlightModel` is `TactileTextsSchemaV1.HighlightModel`, shared unchanged by
   `TactileTextsSchemaV2` (`Packages/ReaderStore/Sources/ReaderStore/Schema.swift`), and V1 is frozen.
   A stored text source needs a `TactileTextsSchemaV3` with a new `HighlightModel` (an optional column)
   and a lightweight stage in `TactileTextsMigrationPlan`; it cannot be added to the V1 class.
5. **Backup.** Highlights travel as `Codable` `Highlight` values in `books/<id>/records.json`
   (`BookRecordsFile`, `Packages/ReaderBackup/Sources/ReaderBackup/BackupManifest.swift`), with
   synthesised `Codable` (`Packages/ReaderCore/Sources/ReaderCore/Model/Records.swift`). The new
   field must be an `Optional` (decoded with `decodeIfPresent`) so `v1-sample.ttbackup`
   (`GoldenFixtureTests.readsTheV1Fixture`) and existing libraries load; a non-optional field
   with a default breaks synthesised decoding. `BookMerge.isDuplicate` compares page, text and
   rects within 0.5 pt, which suits Live Text highlights unchanged.
6. **Search rects and marks.** `PDFTextSearch.run` takes text from `pdfPage.string`, matches on
   UTF-16 offsets into it and makes hit rects with `pdfPage.selection(for: range)`; recognised
   text needs its own rect path from word boxes, as S2 says. It scans pages linearly from
   `startPage` once, so "a running search picks up pages recognised after it started" needs new
   plumbing in the engine and `SearchModel` (pages behind the scan position are never revisited).
   **The mark on the page is a collision S2's description does not cover:**
   `SearchModel.select` → `ReaderSession.jump(toPage:revealing:mark:)` →
   `ReaderPDFController.revealWithoutRecordingHistory` marks by re-creating a `PDFSelection` from
   the rects with `page.selection(for:)` on the on-screen page and setting
   `highlightedSelections`. On a scanned page this finds no text until PDFKit has analysed it
   (seconds after the jump, if ever), so recognised-text hits will not be marked. A mark drawn
   from rects alone needs a change in `App/Features/Reader/`, which is outside the folders S2
   owns.
7. **Import.** `ImportService.importFiles` (`App/Features/Library/ImportService.swift`) commits
   PDFs in step 3 without waiting for the Convert to PDF sheet, and returns per-file
   `ImportResult`s; `ImportPipeline.check(pdf:…)` (nonisolated, already opens the staged file in
   its own `PDFDocument` and reads the page count) is the natural place to sample for detection.
   Only `LibraryScreen.startImport` and `IncomingDocumentHandler` pass a window
   (`requester`); `-importFixture` (`FixtureImporter.importFixture`) imports with no window,
   leaving conversion requests "unaddressed" for the first window to show. S3's UI tests import
   `scanned.pdf` with `-importFixture`, so the notice needs the same "unaddressed, first window
   shows it" handling or those tests cannot see it. A new PDF imported from the Library is not
   opened (only an existing one is), so "before the book opens" applies to the "Open in" path.
8. **iPadOS 17.** Everything about PDFKit's own recognition above is iOS 27 simulator behaviour.
   Whether iPadOS 17 PDFKit does it at all, and so whether scanned pages can be selected or
   highlighted on the floor, is not known (iOS 17 testing is suspended). **[device]**

## Consequences for S1 to S4

**Can proceed as designed**

- **S1**: scan detection over page samples, recognition state, recognised-page value, store and
  sidecar. The highlight source is an optional field with a V3 schema and optional `Codable`
  (items 4 and 5).
- **S2**: recognition with `VNRecognizeTextRequest` accurate rev 3 at about 200 dpi, one page at a
  time; boxes to page space with the formula in question 8; search reading recognised text when
  the file page has none, with hit rects from word boxes. No availability-gated Vision path is
  needed.
- **S3**: as designed, with the `-importFixture` caveat in item 7.

**Must change**

- **S4 is mostly done by existing code.** Selection, the colour menu, capture (rects and text),
  drawing from rects after relaunch, recolour, delete, Copy, Add as Heading and Select All already
  work on scanned pages once PDFKit has recognised them. S4 should be re-scoped to: tag the
  source by checking the page in the file (never the on-screen document); the page crop renderer
  and the crop in "Highlights on this page", the Notebook, the Markdown ZIP and the Notebook PDF;
  backup and merge of the new field. The "caption from recognised words when Live Text gives no
  text" path has no observed trigger and can be dropped or kept as a fallback only.
- **The design text "Live Text reports text or none" should become "Live Text text is whatever
  PDFKit recognised"**, and the reader's menu on a scanned page offers everything a typed page
  offers (questions 3 and 4).
- **S2 needs a rects-only search mark** (item 6), touching `ReaderPDFController` /
  `ReaderSession.jump(toPage:revealing:mark:)`; assign it to S2 explicitly or to a small reader
  change before S2.
- **S4's UI test can make a highlight from a real selection in the simulator** (Live Text runs
  there), but it must wait for PDFKit's recognition (poll the on-screen page's
  `numberOfCharacters`, up to 10 to 15 s on a cold start) before selecting.

**Unverified until a real iPad is used (device-checklist items for S4 to add)**

- PDFKit's automatic recognition happens on the iPad (and which iPads), how long after a page is
  shown, and whether it is affected by Low Power Mode or thermal state.
- The same on **iPadOS 17.0**: if PDFKit does not recognise scanned pages there, selection and
  highlights on scanned pages do not exist on the floor.
- A finger long-press selects text on a scanned page; Highlight, Copy, Look Up (opens the
  dictionary), Add as Heading and Select All work on it.
- A scanned-page highlight is drawn immediately after relaunch, before the page is selectable.
- Vision accurate recognition time per page and peak memory on the iPad at 150, 200 and 300 dpi,
  and battery and heat over a whole book; the import notice's estimate should use these.
- Recognition on real scans: skewed, noisy, two-column, JPEG/JBIG2, rotated (`/Rotate`) pages
  and crop boxes not at the origin.
- Scrolling and markup stay smooth while recognition runs (principle 3).
